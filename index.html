Ohm’s Law — the language behind the circuit

Voltage is the electrical push, current is the rate of charge flow, and resistance opposes that flow. Change one quantity and the others respond predictably.

V
I
R

A little history

Georg Simon Ohm published the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance in the 1820s. The ohm (Ω) later became the SI unit of electrical resistance. For technicians, the importance is practical: the relationship lets us predict what a circuit should do before we ever touch a meter.

The three core forms

I = V ÷ R
V = I × R
R = V ÷ I

Power connects directly: P = V × I, P = I²R, and P = V²/R.

DC Basics — one direction, clear polarity

Direct current maintains a defined polarity and, in the basic model used in ELT 110, charge flow has one direction through the circuit. This page connects the physical idea to voltage, current, resistance, power, sources, meters, and the history of practical DC systems.

− → +

Electron Flow

In this trainer we use electron-flow theory: electron motion is shown from the negative terminal toward the positive terminal through the external circuit.

⎓

DC Voltage

DC sources have identifiable polarity. Batteries, DC power supplies, photovoltaic sources, and rectified supplies are common examples.

+ Positive− NegativeVDC
W

Power

Electrical power is the rate of energy transfer. For resistive DC circuits, connect Ohm’s Law to P = VI, P = I²R, and P = V²/R.

🧰

Measure It

A voltmeter is connected across two points. An ammeter measures current through a path. Resistance is normally measured with the circuit de-energized.

Alternating voltage and current vary with time and periodically reverse direction. The sine wave below turns frequency, period, RMS, peak, and peak-to-peak voltage into something you can see.

For a pure sine wave: Vpeak = VRMS × √2   •   Vp-p = 2Vpeak   •   T = 1/f. RMS is the effective value used to compare the heating effect of AC with DC in a resistive load.
f

Frequency

Frequency is cycles per second, measured in hertz. A 60 Hz waveform completes 60 cycles each second, so one cycle lasts about 16.67 ms.

φ

Phase

Phase describes angular displacement between waveforms. One complete cycle is 360°. Phase relationships become essential in transformers and polyphase systems.

RMS

Effective Value

For a sinusoid, RMS equals peak divided by √2. A 120 V RMS sine wave reaches about ±169.7 V peak.

↕

Peak-to-Peak

Peak-to-peak spans the full excursion from the positive peak to the negative peak: twice the peak value.

Series RCL Circuit — tune through resonance

Put R, L, and C in one series path. Change source voltage, frequency, resistance, inductance, or capacitance and watch inductive and capacitive reactance oppose one another. At resonance, XL = XC, net reactance approaches zero, impedance is minimum, current is maximum, and power factor approaches 1.

NET REACTANCEX = XL − XC, where XL = 2πfL and XC = 1/(2πfC). Positive X is inductive; negative X is capacitive.
IMPEDANCE|Z| = √(R² + (XL − XC)²). At series resonance the reactive terms cancel and |Z| ≈ R.
RESONANCEf₀ = 1/(2π√LC). At f₀, current is maximum, phase ≈ 0°, and PF ≈ 1. VL and VC can each exceed source voltage even though their phasors cancel.
POWER & PHASEP = I²R, Q = I²(XL−XC), S = VI, PF = P/S. Current lags when XL>XC and leads when XC>XL.
